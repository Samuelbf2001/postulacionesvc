### What is your company going to make? Please describe your product and what it does or will do.

> **ES (borrador de trabajo):**
> Sixteam es una firma de servicios nativa en IA para SMBs de servicios. No vendemos la
> herramienta, y tampoco vendemos manos para operarla: instalamos y operamos la tecnología
> y los agentes que hacen el trabajo.
>
> La herramienta ya dejó de ser el problema. El problema es saber usarla, tener el tiempo
> para usarla, y saber cuándo usarla. Un negocio de servicios hoy puede contratar un CRM,
> un chatbot y cinco automatizaciones por poco dinero. Lo que no puede es configurarlas
> para su realidad, mantenerlas vivas y decidir qué mover cuando los números no dan.
>
> Operamos con dos capas de agentes. Unos ejecutan tareas específicas dentro de la
> operación del cliente — atender leads, mover el pipeline, dar seguimiento, generar
> reportes. Otros corren en loop revisando esa operación y buscando mejora continua: qué
> se está cayendo, qué se puede automatizar, qué hay que ajustar. Los humanos aportamos lo
> que los agentes no tienen: la metodología, la experiencia de haberlo hecho decenas de
> veces, el contexto del negocio del cliente y el conocimiento profundo de cada herramienta.
>
> Cobramos por operación entregada, no por asiento ni por hora. Cada tipo de solicitud
> tiene un costo fijo en créditos según su complejidad. Esto alinea el modelo con la IA:
> cuando nuestros agentes se vuelven más productivos, el margen se expande en lugar de
> destruirse — al revés del SaaS por asiento, donde hacer al cliente más eficiente reduce
> tus ingresos.
>
> El escalamiento no es lineal porque las tareas más repetitivas y de mayor volumen las
> absorben los agentes. No buscamos automatizar el servicio completo de golpe, sino llevar
> operaciones específicas a autonomía end-to-end, una por una. Cada operación que un agente
> completa sin intervención humana deja de consumir tiempo de especialista y se vuelve
> capacidad instalada que sirve a todos los clientes siguientes.
>
> Hoy operamos con SMBs de servicios en Latinoamérica — agencias de viajes, servicios
> profesionales basados en citas, inmobiliaria. El problema no es regional: cualquier
> negocio de servicios en el mundo tiene la misma brecha entre la tecnología disponible y
> su capacidad de usarla. Ahí vamos.

> **EN (listo para pegar):**
> Sixteam is an AI-native services company for service SMBs. We don't sell the tool, and we
> don't sell hands to operate it: we install and run the technology and the agents that do
> the work.
>
> The tool stopped being the problem. The problem is knowing how to use it, having the time
> to use it, and knowing when to use it. A service business today can get a CRM, a chatbot,
> and five automations for very little money. What it can't do is configure them for its
> own reality, keep them alive, and decide what to change when the numbers don't work.
>
> We run two layers of agents. Some execute specific tasks inside the client's operation —
> responding to leads, moving the pipeline, following up, generating reports. Others run in
> loops reviewing that operation and looking for continuous improvement: what's falling
> through, what can be automated, what needs adjusting. Humans bring what the agents don't
> have: the methodology, the experience of having done this dozens of times, the context of
> the client's business, and deep knowledge of each tool.
>
> We charge per operation delivered — not per seat, not per hour. Each type of request has
> a fixed credit cost based on its complexity. This aligns the model with AI: when our
> agents get more productive, our margin expands instead of collapsing — the opposite of
> seat-based SaaS, where making your customer more efficient reduces your revenue.
>
> Scaling isn't linear because the most repetitive, highest-volume tasks get absorbed by
> agents. We're not trying to automate the entire service at once — we're taking specific
> operations to end-to-end autonomy, one at a time. Every operation an agent can complete
> without human intervention stops consuming specialist time and becomes installed capacity
> that serves every client after it.
>
> Today we run this for service SMBs across Latin America — travel agencies,
> appointment-based professional services, real estate. The problem isn't regional: every
> service business in the world has the same gap between the technology available to it and
> its ability to use it. That's where we're going.

>
> AI removed the first barrier — the technology is now cheap. It did not remove the
> second: implementing it, operating it, and making it produce revenue. Our own clients
> proved the demand: they kept asking us to stay after every project. So we stopped
> selling implementations and started selling the operation — and clients converted from

> our strongest demand signal.

### Who are your competitors? What do you understand about your business that they don't?

> Three groups: (1) traditional agencies and freelancers, who implement and leave;
> (2) SaaS tools (HubSpot, GoHighLevel and its thousands of white-label resellers), which
> hand the SMB a tool and wish them luck; (3) emerging AI-agent startups (in LatAm,
> Cifrato in accounting is the closest analog), which mostly sell single-function agents.
>
> What we understand that they don't: for every $1 SMBs spend on software they spend ~$6
> on the services and labor to operate it — the market is the service layer, not the
> tool. SaaS is structurally trapped: seat-based billing means the more productive AI
> makes the customer, the less the vendor earns. Our credit model bills per operation
> completed, so AI productivity gains expand our margin instead of shrinking our revenue.
> And agencies can't compound: they sell hours and re-solve every problem from scratch,
> while every workflow we productize makes the next customer cheaper to serve. We're
> built as the thing Sequoia and YC have both described: a software company that looks
> like a services firm.
